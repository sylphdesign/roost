Connect Gmail

Google blocks its normal sign-in inside desktop apps, so you make your own free Google key once. We walk you through it. Nobody else sees your mail.

Step 1 of 4about 10 min left

  1. Open Google Cloud Console and create a new project. Any name works, for example Roost.

  2. Check that your new project is selected at the top of the page, then click Enable.

  3. In Google Auth Platform, choose External, then type an app name and your email. Under Audience, add each Gmail address you will use in Roost as a Test user. Leave Data Access empty: Roost asks for Gmail access when you sign in.

  4. Click Create client, choose Application type Desktop app, then click Create. In the box that opens, click Download JSON.

    One key works for all your Gmail accounts. While the app is in Testing mode Google asks you to sign in again every 7 days.

Sign in to Gmail

Your browser opens so you can sign in with Google. Then come back here.

Google hasn’t verified this app The app is requesting access to sensitive info in your Google Account… Advanced · Go to Roost (unsafe)
Coming up: you will see this page. It is normal. It is your own key, so Google has not checked it. Click Advanced, then Go to … (unsafe), then Continue, and tick every box. Your mail stays between your Mac and Google.

Finish signing in in your browser…

If Google says “Access blocked … has not completed the Google verification process”, add this Gmail address under Test users in your Google Cloud project, then try again. If Google says “Google hasn’t verified this app”, that is normal for your own key: click Continue (or Advanced → Go to … (unsafe), then Continue). Tick every permission box.
